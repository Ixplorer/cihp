History & Evolution

From Global Partnership to Indigenous Powerhouse

CIHP was established in 2010 as part of a global movement to transfer donor-supported health program management to local indigenous institutions.

2006 – 2010: Operational Foundations

ICAP at Columbia University Footprint

Before CIHP’s formal establishment, operations functioned under ICAP at Columbia University (formerly the International Center for AIDS Care and Treatment Programs) in Nigeria, building clinical infrastructure, training healthcare workers, and expanding PEPFAR-funded HIV/AIDS interventions.

2010: Indigenous Transition

Establishment of CIHP as an Indigenous NGO

In 2010, CIHP transitioned directly from ICAP to become a fully registered indigenous Nigerian non-profit organization. Dr. Bolanle Oyeledun, former ICAP Country Director, led the newly autonomous organization to foster local ownership, enhance funding stewardship, and strengthen sustainable health delivery.

2010 – 2022: Multi-State Scale & Grant Stewardship

Expansion Across 20 Nigerian States

CIHP expanded its technical portfolio from HIV/AIDS prevention to comprehensive Maternal & Child Health (RMNCH), Tuberculosis control, laboratory networks, and digital health surveillance. The organization secured and successfully administered major multi-year grants from PEPFAR (via the CDC), European Union (EU), GAVI (The Vaccine Alliance), The Global Fund, and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.

2025 – 2026 & Beyond: Global Consortia & Pan-African Expansion

Catalytic Innovation & Multi-Country Leadership

In 2025/2026, CIHP launched the flagship REACHOUT Project (funded by the European Union / EDCTP3 in consortium across Italy, Belgium, Germany, Switzerland, Rwanda, and Kenya), the IMPAACT4HIV Project (funded by Unitaid for pediatric AHD & TB care), the AEARI Project (scaling PPH E-MOTIVE bundles across Lagos State), and its 4th Gates Foundation Award strengthening newborn care units nationwide—while advancing a 3-year strategic ambition to establish 5 Pan-African operational footholds.

Institutional Leadership

Executive Management & Governance

CIHP is led by distinguished public health leaders dedicated to organizational integrity, clinical excellence, and Pan-African growth.

Dr. Bolanle Oyeledun

Chief Executive Officer & Member, Board of Directors

Dr. Bolanle Oyeledun has served as Chief Executive Officer of CIHP since 2013, having previously led ICAP Nigeria from 2006 to 2011. Under her visionary leadership, CIHP expanded from a donor transition initiative into a recognized indigenous health powerhouse reaching over 11 million lives across 20 Nigerian states.

  • Education: MBBS (University of Ibadan, 1987), MPH (University of Lagos, 1995)
  • Focus Areas: Public Health Systems, HIV/AIDS Programmatic Scaling, Maternal Health
  • Governance Role: Executive Oversight & Pan-African Strategy Implementation
